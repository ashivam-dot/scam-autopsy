# Scam Autopsy: sourced case files, built for repeat viewing

Scam Autopsy is an English-language case-file channel that explains the exact turn where a scam becomes dangerous and gives viewers one action they can use. The ambition is 10 million subscribers, treated as a long-term direction rather than a forecast. The starting channel is tiny (4 subscribers; eight Shorts observed at roughly 3–46 views), so current view counts cannot establish which topic or format works. The immediate goal is to earn trust and find repeatable audience interest with sourced, distinctive stories.

## Editorial standard

Every factual claim in the narration must be traceable to a primary source. Each script needs a source URL, the source's date, and a short reviewer note identifying the passage that supports the hook and any number. Use an identifiable incident only when the source documents one. Otherwise call it an FBI-documented **pattern** or **warning**, and avoid presenting an invented character as a real victim. Quote scam messages only when the source quotes them; invented message screens are labeled **Illustration**. Round a sourced amount only in a direction the source supports: the August 2025 wire was **over $449,000**, and the report says the full amount was **on hold**, not recovered or returned. Do not invent names, loss amounts, screenshots, timelines, arrests, or outcomes for dramatic effect.

Use a direct opening that contains a concrete turn: the wrong wire account, the QR code on an unordered parcel, the payment demanded to unlock a job, or the “safe” account instruction. Within 35–55 seconds, deliver: hook; setup; mechanism; the moment of trust transfer; documented result or scale; an independently verifiable protective action; and a concise source cue. Give every episode a materially different visual argument and narrative rhythm. Text on screen should be sparse and legible on a phone. Fictional interfaces, messages, people, receipts, or locations should carry an **Illustration** label. Put the source link and “FBI-documented incident,” “FBI-documented pattern,” or “FTC-documented pattern,” as appropriate, in the description. A citation is editorial transparency; it does not give permission to copy the source's prose or someone else's footage.

The first six reviewed Shorts in `content/` are the initial release queue. After each two Shorts, inspect retention, “viewed versus swiped,” and comments manually in YouTube Studio; the automated reports track views, engaged views, viewing averages, and subscriber change, but do not collect those Studio-only details or comment text. Change the hook, pacing, or visual explanation only when observations justify it. Low reach at the starting scale is weak evidence. The implemented schedule publishes at most one researched Short on Tuesday and one on Saturday. Long-form is a future expansion, not an automatic output of this studio: after short-form signals identify a strong topic, plan a 5–8 minute deep dive with original evidence, source interpretation, and a precise prevention walkthrough. Connect that video to related Shorts when produced. Reassess after 20–30 properly sourced Shorts; retention and returning viewers matter more than a calendar-driven subscriber promise.

## YouTube policy and automation constraints checked 8 October 2026

- [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) say reviewers assess the channel as a whole. Mass-produced, generic, minimally varied scripts and AI templates lacking original insight can fail the *inauthentic content* policy. Reused material requires significant original commentary or substantive change, separately from copyright permission. The same page now says AI personas that present themselves as human experts offering financial, legal, health, or political advice are ineligible for monetization. Use a consistent channel narrator without presenting a synthetic persona as a human financial adviser or other expert.
- [YouTube's GenAI disclosure rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) require disclosure when realistic generated or meaningfully altered content makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not, alters a real event or place, or creates a realistic scene that did not occur. Script assistance, captions, and clearly non-realistic animation do not themselves require disclosure. The uploader currently sets the synthetic-media disclosure on every video; a visible **Illustration** label also protects editorial clarity. YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit audience or monetization eligibility.
- [YPP eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en): ad-revenue entry requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public long-form watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified public Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts Feed hours do not count toward the 4,000 hours. Thresholds trigger review, not automatic approval. [Expanded YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en) in eligible regions, including India, offers earlier fan-funding/Shopping entry at 500 subscribers, three public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. This is not ad-revenue entry.
- The current [Shorts monetization page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en) says that starting 1 February 2027, a monetizing partner needs at least 10 million qualified Shorts views to earn a share of that month's Shorts Creator Pool ads and YouTube Premium revenue. For each earnings month, YouTube measures the 90-day window ending on the 15th of the preceding calendar month. Recheck this policy before relying on future revenue estimates. It also excludes non-original and artificial views.
- The live [YouTube Data API `videos.insert` reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/insert) documents `status.containsSyntheticMedia`, states a quota impact of 100 calls per day at one unit per upload call, and explicitly says videos uploaded from unverified API projects are not restricted to private viewing mode. The [quota page](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost) states a default 100-call daily `videos.insert` bucket and a separate default 10,000-unit daily bucket for other endpoints. This studio still uploads privately first so it can verify processing before publication. Check actual Cloud Console limits, because quotas can change or be project-specific. [Google OAuth guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2) says an external-consent app in Testing normally receives refresh tokens expiring in seven days for YouTube upload scope. Publishing status, consent verification, and token storage must be handled explicitly in a durable cloud workflow.

The six seed Shorts were checked directly against FBI/IC3 records before approval. The bounded FTC refill applies automated gates: an allowed primary-source URL and extractable article body; exact, contiguous evidence quotes for scenes; digit-based numerical claims matched to those quotes; and a second, different Gemini model reviewing factual fidelity, advice, quality, variety, and invented-incident risk. Accepted scripts carry source and review metadata; rejected candidates remain unpublished. These gates reduce error but cannot guarantee factual accuracy, so spot-check generated scripts when possible. Human review is not a required step in the automated publishing path. No source supports a guaranteed reach, subscriber count, or revenue outcome. Keep narration and graphics original and respect rights for any image or audio.
