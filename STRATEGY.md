# Scam Autopsy: sourced case files, built for repeat viewing

Scam Autopsy is an English-language case-file channel that explains the exact turn where a scam becomes dangerous and gives viewers one action they can use. The ambition is 10 million subscribers, treated as a long-term direction rather than a forecast. The starting channel is tiny (4 subscribers; eight Shorts observed at roughly 3–46 views), so current view counts cannot establish which topic or format works. The immediate goal is to earn trust and find repeatable audience interest with sourced, distinctive stories.

## Editorial standard

Every factual claim in the narration must be traceable to a primary source. Each script needs a source URL, the source's date, and a short reviewer note identifying the passage that supports the hook and any number. Use an identifiable incident only when the source documents one. Otherwise call it an FBI-documented **pattern** or **warning**, and avoid presenting an invented character as a real victim. Quote scam messages only when the source quotes them; invented message screens are labeled **Illustration**. Round a sourced amount only in a direction the source supports: the August 2025 wire was **over $449,000**, and the report says the full amount was **on hold**, not recovered or returned. Do not invent names, loss amounts, screenshots, timelines, arrests, or outcomes for dramatic effect.

Use a direct opening that contains a concrete turn: the wrong wire account, the QR code on an unordered parcel, the payment demanded to unlock a job, or the “safe” account instruction. Within 35–50 seconds, deliver: hook; setup; mechanism; the moment of trust transfer; documented result or scale; an independently verifiable protective action; and a concise source cue. Give every episode a materially different visual argument and narrative rhythm. Text on screen should be sparse and legible on a phone. Fictional interfaces, messages, people, receipts, or locations should carry an **Illustration** label. Put the source link and “FBI-documented incident” or “FBI-documented pattern” in the description. A citation is editorial transparency; it does not give permission to copy the source's prose or someone else's footage.

The first six reviewed Shorts in `content/` are the initial release queue. After each two Shorts, inspect the first seconds of retention and “viewed versus swiped,” comments that reveal confusion, and subscribers gained per 1,000 views. Change the hook, pacing, or visual explanation only when those observations justify it. Low reach at the starting scale is weak evidence. Maintain a sustainable cadence of two or three researched Shorts per week. Release one 5–8 minute deep dive every two to four weeks around the case or mechanism that showed the strongest viewer interest. Long videos should add original evidence, source interpretation, and a precise prevention walkthrough, rather than stretching a Short. Use the deep dive's related Short links, end screen, and description to connect the formats. Reassess after 20–30 properly sourced Shorts; retention and returning viewers matter more than a calendar-driven subscriber promise.

## Twelve evergreen episode leads

The first six are in `content/`. Leads 2–6 and 8–12 are documented *patterns* unless noted; avoid implying that the advisory provides a particular victim's full story.

## YouTube policy and automation constraints checked 8 October 2026

- [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) say reviewers assess the channel as a whole. Mass-produced, generic, minimally varied scripts and AI templates lacking original insight can fail the *inauthentic content* policy. Reused material requires significant original commentary or substantive change, separately from copyright permission. The same page now says AI personas that present themselves as human experts offering financial, legal, health, or political advice are ineligible for monetization. Use an identifiable channel narrator, not a synthetic banker or financial adviser persona.
- [YouTube's GenAI disclosure rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) require disclosure when realistic generated or meaningfully altered content makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not, alters a real event or place, or creates a realistic scene that did not occur. Script assistance, captions, and clearly non-realistic animation do not themselves require disclosure. If a reenactment looks real, set the upload's AI-use disclosure; a visible **Illustration** label also protects editorial clarity. YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit audience or monetization eligibility.
- [YPP eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en): ad-revenue entry requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public long-form watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified public Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts Feed hours do not count toward the 4,000 hours. Thresholds trigger review, not automatic approval. [Expanded YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en) in eligible regions, including India, offers earlier fan-funding/Shopping entry at 500 subscribers, three public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. This is not ad-revenue entry.
- The current [Shorts monetization page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en) says that starting 1 February 2027, a monetizing partner needs 10 million qualified Shorts views in the preceding 90 days to earn that month's Shorts ad and YouTube Premium share. Recheck this policy before relying on future revenue estimates. It also excludes non-original and artificial views.
- The live [YouTube Data API `videos.insert` reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/insert) documents `status.containsSyntheticMedia` and, as checked, a default 100-upload daily bucket with one unit per upload. It says unverified projects' uploads are not limited to private viewing. The [quota page](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost) currently agrees on the 100-upload bucket and says other endpoints share a default 10,000-unit daily bucket. Check actual Cloud Console limits, because quotas can change or be project-specific. [Google OAuth guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2) says an external-consent app in Testing normally receives refresh tokens expiring in seven days for YouTube upload scope. Publishing status, consent verification, and token storage must be handled explicitly in a durable cloud workflow.

No source supports a guaranteed reach or subscriber outcome. Keep narration and graphics original, respect platform rights for any image/audio, and perform a human source review before automatic publication.
